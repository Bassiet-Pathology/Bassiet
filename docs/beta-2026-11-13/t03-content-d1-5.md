## 1. Verdict — score 7/10

The medicine is very accurate. Across about 7,300 words of text and 110 questions I found only a handful of real factual errors. The worst are the hydroxyurea mechanism, metronidazole offered for Actinomyces, cat-scratch disease called "non-caseating", and a wrong clostridial toxin. No answer key is wrong.

Two things pull the score down:
- **The stain tests can be passed without reading.** 76 of 110 answers are option 2, and the options are never shuffled. Pressing "2" every time stains 20 of the 22 chapters at 60% or more. In my run this alone broke the Depth 1–3 seals.
- **Coverage is uneven.** Depths 1–3 read like a compact textbook. Depths 4–5 are mostly tables with little prose: c4-deficient has 143 words of prose and c4-hyper has 156. Several of the most tested Step 1 blocks are missing from every chapter, including antimicrobial pharmacology, lysosomal storage diseases, the urea cycle, purine/pyrimidine metabolism, the HMP shunt, fatty-acid oxidation, mycobacteria, bacterial genetics and mutation types.

On phones, every table is forced to at least 480 px wide, so the last column is cut off: the treatment column, the "features" column, the disease column.

## 2. What works well

- Accuracy is high and up to date in most places: Sepsis-3 septic shock definition; MAC prophylaxis "no longer routine if ART started"; ceftriaxone plus doxycycline for gonorrhoea; IV artesunate; the current HIV testing algorithm; elexacaftor-tezacaftor-ivacaftor; oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin for C. difficile; HLA-B*57:01 and B*15:02.
- No answer key is wrong. Distractors are plausible, and explanations agree with the text.
- Stains are used well. The cross-subject callouts are the best part: digoxin and hypokalaemia; catalase and CGD; arsenic and lipoic acid; aflatoxin and TP53; Malassezia and azelaic acid. They render as clear coloured blocks with a subject label, and the REMEMBER boxes stand out.
- Tables are well chosen. The repair-crew table, the necrosis patterns, the repeat expansions, the HIV CD4 ladder, the transfusion reactions and the worm table are all genuinely high-yield.
- The prose in Depths 1–2 (membrane, furnace, unmaking, burning front) has real mechanism and reads like a short textbook, not a list.
- Every chapter's concept links (`c:[]`) resolve to Atlas concepts (checked with cids.js).

## 4. Top 5 improvement ideas

1. **Make the stain tests honest and Step-1-shaped.** Shuffle options (t03-01) and rebalance the keys. Then turn at least 2 of each chapter's 5 questions into two-step vignettes: give the finding, ask about the mechanism or the next drug. Step 1 rarely asks "which enzyme?"; it asks "this patient's drug acts on…". Write every `x` to cover each distractor (t03-18), so the Hull and Yuzu cards teach.
2. **Add the missing Step 1 blocks as new strata.** Depth 5 has the room and the lore for "The Arsenal" (antimicrobials, t03-09) and "Acid-fast rods / How bacteria share weapons" (t03-11, t03-13). Depth 1 or 3 needs "The Side Doors" (HMP shunt, urea cycle, fatty-acid oxidation, nucleotides) and "The Locked Rooms" (lysosomal storage) (t03-10). These are the five blocks a Step 1 student will most miss.
3. **Raise Depths 4–5 to the prose depth of Depths 1–2** (t03-17). Each table should be introduced by one paragraph of mechanism that explains why the table's patterns exist. For example: why T-cell defects give fungi and viruses; why complement is low in type III; why Gram-negative sepsis gives DIC.
4. **Interactive figures for Depths 4–5**, which today have one figure (gram) across 10 chapters. High-value candidates: complement cascade (toggle a deficiency and see the infection or disease), MHC I/II antigen-loading paths, HIV life cycle with drug targets clickable, HBV serology over time (slider through acute, window period, recovery, chronic), and the Plasmodium life cycle with primaquine's hypnozoite target.
5. **A phone-first table design** (t03-08). Tables carry most of the high-yield content; on a 390 px screen they should become stacked "cards" (row = card, column name as a small mono label). A "compare" tap could then send any book table straight into Practice › Compare.

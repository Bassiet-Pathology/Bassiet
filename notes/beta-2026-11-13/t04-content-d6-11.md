## 1. Verdict — score 7/10

The medicine in depths 6–11 is mostly accurate and up to date: SGLT2 inhibitors in heart failure, a mean pulmonary pressure cut-off of >20 mmHg, colorectal screening from age 45, triple CFTR modulators, andexanet and idarucizumab, the 36-hour gap for sacubitril-valsartan. I found only a handful of real factual slips. The biggest problem is the stain tests, not the text. **103 of the 135 answers are option 2.** The app shows options in source order, so pressing "2" every time passes 24 of the 27 chapters at the 60% threshold. One question (c9-exchange Q1) uses numbers that are physiologically impossible. Teaching depth is good but thin in places: many sections are a dense list or a table with a one-line lead-in. 54 of 135 explanations are 35 characters or fewer and say only the diagnosis. Several high-yield Step 1 items are missing from the whole app, most notably α-thalassaemia.

## 2. What works well

- The facts are dense and correct. Tables such as the Vaughan Williams classes, the RTA types, the nephritic and nephrotic diseases, IBD, the shock haemodynamics and the PV-loop changes match current Step 1 teaching.
- Guidance is current: no SABA-only advice for COPD, ESA haemoglobin target ≤115 g/L, HFrEF four-pillar therapy, CRC screening at 45, latent TB regimens (4R, 9H, 3HP), and MASH-era drugs such as GLP-1 agonists listed as a cause of pancreatitis.
- The stain callouts do link subjects well (for example cholera and pertussis toxin on the G-protein table, B12 enzymes on the megaloblastic page, the CFTR mechanism by tissue).
- The REMEMBER boxes pick the right one-liners ("Loops lose calcium; thiazides keep it", "Normal A–a gradient hypoxaemia: altitude or hypoventilation").
- Every concept id in the `c:` arrays resolves to a BANK concept, and every answer index is in range.

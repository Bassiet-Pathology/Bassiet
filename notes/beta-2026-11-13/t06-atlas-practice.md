# t06: Atlas and Practice (bank1–8, app-today.js Atlas, app-practice.js)

## 1. Verdict: 6/10

Most of the bank's medicine is accurate. I checked all 155 lenses, 296 links, 57 chains, 40 predict scenarios and 38 compare tables, and found only a few real content errors. Two of them are clear-cut: the pertussis pearl in `ch_cholera` and the lithium + thiazide predict row. The bigger problem is how the practice modes score answers:
- **Compare gives the answer away.** The options always appear in column order, so "pick the option at the column's position" scores 94% (574 of 609 questions) without knowing anything.
- **Trace scores by exact position.** If one step is moved, the whole chain is marked wrong and graded "again". Several chains also have steps that happen in parallel, so their order is genuinely ambiguous.
- **Atlas links read badly.** About 30 of the 296 link sentences read wrongly or ungrammatically. Most come from three concept names: `autoimmunity` is named "Lupus and autoantibodies", `potassium` is "Potassium disorders", and `copd` is "Obstructive lung disease".

The UI itself is clean on phone and desktop, and there were no JS errors in any session.

Screenshots are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/c80d3a2e-1d1d-5dac-83ee-172c002ca966/scratchpad/beta/shots/t06-*.png`. Scripts and content dumps are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/c80d3a2e-1d1d-5dac-83ee-172c002ca966/scratchpad/beta/work-t06/`.

## 2. What works well
- The bank is clean as data: every link, chain, predict and compare item points at a concept that exists, there are no duplicate ids or self-loops, and every concept has at least one link.
- Lenses are dense and in original wording, and they are accurate. The renal, endocrine and heme lenses are strong.
- On phone, Trace by tapping works smoothly. The given first step is clearly marked, and Undo works. After you check, the correct order, the key point and links to related concepts all appear.
- Predict has large arrow buttons (48×44 on phone), and Check stays disabled until every row has an answer. After checking, the right answer shows in green and your wrong pick in red.
- On desktop the Atlas splits into a sticky list and a detail pane. `/` focuses the search box, and the search text survives opening a concept.
- Due items are scheduled with a simple SM-2-style formula. Misses are logged to the Hull automatically. A session survives switching tabs.

## 4. Top 5 improvements (bigger changes)
1. **Make scores honest.** Shuffle Compare options (t06-29), score Trace by adjacent pairs and longest increasing subsequence (t06-30), add more no-change Predict rows (t06-39), and use three or more options on Compare. Until then, the spaced-repetition schedule and the Hull are fed by noise.
2. **Run one link-label pass.** Read every edge aloud as "From label To", rename the three problem concepts (Autoimmunity and lupus, Potassium, COPD and emphysema), and reverse the backwards edges. This removes about 30 confusing sentences in one sitting.
3. **Give sessions a beginning and an end.** Cap sessions at 8, put a due entry point on the Dive home, and end with a summary card showing score by mode, the missed items with links, and when they return. Optionally fold the summary into the dive story (for example, a "trail log").
4. **Make the Atlas map useful on phone.** Use a vertical "constellation" with readable 14 px nodes coloured by system and arrows for direction. Tapping an edge should show its label sentence. Keep the list scroll position when going back.
5. **Close coverage gaps.** Every concept should have at least one practice item, and microbiology and pharmacology need more chains and tables. Allow order-flexible step groups in chains (`steps:[…,['A','B'],…]`, scored as an unordered set) so physiology that happens in parallel can be taught honestly.
